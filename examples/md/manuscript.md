# Green roofs and cooling loads in terraced housing

## 1 Introduction

Rooftop vegetation has been studied as a passive cooling measure for several decades [3]. Early field campaigns reported surface temperature reductions of more than 20 K on sunny afternoons [1,2], although the reductions measured inside buildings were far smaller [5]. Sedum roofs are known to tolerate drought better than grass roofs. Irrigation raises the benefit further but increases water demand [4]. Recent work has combined sensor networks with simulation to estimate savings at neighbourhood scale [7], and two independent teams reached similar conclusions [3, 4, 5, 6]. Little is known about terraced housing in humid climates.

## 2 Methods

We instrumented 12 terraced houses in the fictional town of Alder Vale during 2023. Indoor air temperature was logged every 10 minutes. Substrate moisture followed the protocol of Okafor and Lindqvist [2]. Cooling energy was estimated from compressor run time following the method described in [8].

## 3 Results

Green-roof houses used 14% less cooling energy than matched controls over the summer. The effect was largest during heatwaves and smallest in the shoulder months. A sensitivity analysis showed that the result is robust to the choice of control houses [9].
